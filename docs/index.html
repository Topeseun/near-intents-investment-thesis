NEAR Hackathon 2026 · Investment Thesis track

NEAR is becoming a cross-chain settlement business. Price it like one.

NEAR’s base layer barely earns anything — its Intents layer already grossed $5,578,208 of fees in 30 days, keeps a growing share for NEAR buybacks, and is the only part of NEAR with a compounding, measurable revenue curve. The long-term investment case is not “cheap fast L1” but “toll road on cross-chain volume”: value NEAR on retained Intents revenue (fee-capture DCF), with base-layer usage and staking economics as the supporting chapters that test whether the toll road has a moat.

Honest verdict up front: at today’s fees and the measured 36.0% capture rate, retained revenue alone supports $0.42 per token — 11.8% of the $3.60 price. The case for owning NEAR is the option on fee growth and rising capture, not today’s cash flow. Everything below is built to test that option, not to sell it.

The model in one paragraph

Value = R1 ÷ (r − g), where R0 = trailing-30d Intents gross fees × 12 × capture rate, R1 = R0 × (1 + g), and value per token = Value ÷ circulating supply (1,249,836,992 NEAR). Base case: R0 = $24,077,777/yr, R1 = $26,485,555, equity value $529.71M → $0.4238/token at r=15%, g=10%, measured capture 36.0%. The 10% growth rate is a forward assumption (see src/assumptions.yaml), not a trend extrapolation: trailing-30d fees are down -30.5% vs the prior 30 days and the 365-day log-regression growth is ≈ −0.7%. The full-series log-regression is dominated by the May–Aug 2025 launch ramp ($1/day → ~$200k/day) and is not quoted anywhere in this thesis as a growth rate.

Network activity — the usage chapter in one paragraph

Base-layer usage is the thesis’s bear leg, and it is now measured on a full daily series (NEARBlocks /v1/charts, 2,303 days from 2020-07-21): 895,302 transactions and 77,752 active accounts on 2026-11-09 — respectively 93.6% and 96.6% below their all-time peaks (13,935,402 txns on 2023-12-01; 2,256,512 accounts on 2024-04-22). New accounts are slowing as well (287,194 in 30 days, -18.9% vs the prior 30). Over the last 365 days, weekly changes in NEAR’s price and in Intents fees moved together with a Pearson correlation of 0.06 (n=366 weeks; price vs base-layer gas fees: 0.57) — a co-movement, not a lead-lag result and not evidence of causation. The investment question is therefore not whether NEAR’s base layer is busy (it is not, relative to its peaks) but whether Intents volume — $3.71B in 30 days at a 15.02 bps fee rate — keeps compounding while the base layer does not.

Capture is rising — the one trend that matters

Retained ÷ gross fees by window (revenue.near.org, measured from its server-rendered payload, cross-checked against the per-mechanism split): all-time 19.1% → YTD 23.0% → 7d 32.2% → 30d 36.0%. 30d split: direct fees 21.4% + B2B 10.9% + quote improvement 3.7%. The model’s base case uses the 30d rate, not the lifetime rate.

Limitations — what this thesis cannot show

  • Daily activity is now measured — and it is weak. The NEARBlocks /v1/charts endpoint (2,303 daily points from 21 Jul 2020) gives daily transactions, active accounts and new accounts; the research-phase claim that “no daily endpoint exists” was wrong — the bare /v1/charts path was never tested, only suffixed paths (all 404). Daily transactions are 93.6% below their 2023-12-01 peak and daily active accounts 96.6% below their 2024-04-22 peak. Early rows have gaps: multichain_txns is null before 14 Nov 2024 and txn_volume is null on 11 days in Jul–Aug 2020; neither field is used in any metric.
  • Volume is now measured too — with a shorter window. DefiLlama summary/dexs/near-intents gives a daily Intents volume series (699 points from 10 Dec 2024), so the growth story finally has its own x-axis: $3.71B in 30 days, implying a 15.02 bps gross fee rate. The volume series starts Dec 2024, not at the May 2025 fee-series start — the two series cover different windows, and the endpoint’s own 30d total ($3.56B) differs slightly from the sum of the last 30 daily points because its rolling window ends at fetch time, after the last daily point.
  • 365-day price cap. CoinGecko’s free tier limits history to 365 days (days=max returns 401). All price history here is one year.
  • 24-hour fee anomaly. Intents fees for 9 Nov 2026 were $16,790 vs a 7-day daily average of $185,884 — a full-day value in the series, so either a real one-day drop or a DefiLlama reporting lag. No single day is ever annualised; all annualisation uses 7d/30d trailing figures.
  • Fees are volatile, not a ramp. Trailing-30d fees peaked at $8,236,902 on 2026-10-14 and are 32.3% below that peak. Do not quote the full-series launch-ramp CAGR.
  • Revenue-dashboard caveats. The revenue.near.org headline revenue figure ($11.54M) has an unlabelled window in the page payload and is never used as a model input. Its wallet table is partial: the live NEARBlocks balance of buybacks.multisignature.near (2,229,769 NEAR) is higher than the dashboard’s traced all-time inflow (1,719,606 NEAR), so it must not be read as the full buyback total.
  • TVL endpoints disagree. DefiLlama’s NEAR historical-series last point ($171.06M) vs its /chains headline ($365.22M). The auditable series value is used.
  • Peer fees undercount. DefiLlama chain-fee totals can miss revenue other methodologies include (notably Solana). Benchmarks rank within this dataset only.
  • Intents flow is concentrated in one routing provider. Faultline (a fellow Investment Thesis entry) measured from NearStats routing data, 30 days to 8 Nov 2026, that a single provider, SwapKit, carried $260.99M of $765.65M observed 1Click volume (34.1%, across 48 apps). An outage or pause at that provider is a concentration risk to the Intents flow — and therefore to the fee capture this thesis values: its simulation displaces 10.2% of total observed volume even if 70% of SwapKit’s flow reroutes. The Oct 1 service pause is the live precedent: Oct 1–4 volume ran 31.6% below the prior matching week.
